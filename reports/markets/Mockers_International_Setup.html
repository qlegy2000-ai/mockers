The percentage is one setting per market, not per product — Settings → Markets → [market] → Products and pricing. Changing it later is one field, never a re-pricing job. The EGP list stays the reference, so a base price change still flows through to every market.

Verified liveChecked against the real price per country: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Germany and Sweden all return 1.25×; Egypt returns 1.00×.
A trap worth recording. A new market catalogue only auto-publishes products created after it exists, and a price list adjusts only what is inside its catalogue. Both new catalogues started empty, so the +25% applied to nothing and shoppers would have seen the plain Egyptian price. All 401 active products were published into both catalogues — 0 errors — and prices were then re-checked per country rather than trusted.
Prices are not rounded. 679 × 1.25 = 848.75 and that is what shows. Say the word if you want them rounded to a tidier figure.

Figures above are for a 1,998.75 EGP hoodie. Small amounts keep two decimals, larger ones round, so the number always reads naturally.

Why it can only be an estimate

Shopify ties true multi-currency to Shopify Payments, which is not available in Egypt. Verified on the store: no Shopify Payments account exists, the only presentment currency is EGP, and the storefront reports a conversion rate of 1.0. There is no setting that changes this, on any plan.

The customer is always charged in EGP. On a product page the line reads ≈ SAR 140 · charged in EGP, and every estimate carries a tooltip saying so. Being blunt about it matters: a converted number that quietly turns back into EGP at checkout is how a sale is lost at the final step.
Rates are static and need refreshing, roughly monthly. They were taken on 12 Nov 2026 from a public feed. Nothing is fetched at page load, so no third party sees your traffic and nothing slows the site. Drift is cosmetic, since the charge is in EGP either way.

One correction made

Bulgaria was initially mapped to the lev. It adopted the euro on 1 January 2026 and the lev stopped being legal tender that February, so a Bulgarian shopper would have been shown a dead currency. Corrected to EUR and verified live.

5

Cash on Delivery closed outside Egypt

COD is not honoured on international orders, so it had to stop being offered. It is roughly 70% of the business — 50 of the last 71 orders — which made this the most delicate change of the set.

The rule ACTIVE

Shopify exposes no per-country control over payment methods, so this needed the Payment Rules app. The rule now live:

FieldSetting
NameNO COD
TypeHide
MethodCash on Delivery (COD)
ConditionCountry is not Egypt (EG)
The condition is deliberately "is not Egypt" rather than a list of foreign countries. A list would silently let COD through the day a new market opens; this phrasing never does. The method name also matches your live checkout exactly — orders record the gateway as Cash on Delivery (COD), character for character.

A notice in the cart as well

International shoppers now see a line directly above the checkout button: "International orders are card payment only — Cash on Delivery is not available outside Egypt." Egypt sees nothing. It explains the absence rather than leaving people guessing, and it covered the gap before the app rule existed.

Still to confirm TEST

The app's preview cannot detect COD, because Shopify classes it as a manual payment method — which is why the preview says "rule does not apply here". That is not a failure, but it is not proof either. Only a real checkout proves it, and Egypt must be tested first. Watch one specific thing: before a customer types an address, the country is unknown, and if the app reads unknown as "not Egypt", COD could vanish for Egyptian shoppers until they fill the address in. Also worth checking whether the app matches on shipping or billing country — if billing, an Egyptian paying with a foreign card could lose COD on a domestic delivery.

Paymob remains available everywhere, so foreign customers still have a way to pay. Worth confirming with Paymob that it accepts non-Egyptian cards — if it does not, those 42 countries would have no payment method at all.

Testing any country without a VPN

Shopify picks the market from the visitor's IP, so checking a country would normally mean a VPN. A hidden switcher was added instead.

ActionHow
Show the panelmockers.shop/?mktest=1 — remembered on that browser
Switch countryPick from the dropdown; the page reloads in that market
Hide it againClick ×, or mockers.shop/?mktest=0
Customers cannot see it. It renders hidden on every page and only appears for someone who types the URL; the flag lives in that one browser. The dropdown lists 43 countries — the sheet's 42 plus Egypt — read live from your open markets, so the list checks itself: a country missing from it is a country that is closed.

Prices and the currency estimate follow the switch immediately. Shipping is only visible at checkout, so add to cart and go as far as the shipping step, then stop. This panel is temporary — say the word and it is removed.
