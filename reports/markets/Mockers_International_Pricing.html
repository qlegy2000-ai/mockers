A trap worth recording

A new market catalogue only auto-publishes products created after it exists, and a price list adjusts only what is inside its catalogue. Both new catalogues started empty, so the +25% applied to nothing and shoppers would have seen the plain Egyptian price. All 401 active products were published into both catalogues — 0 errors — and the prices were then re-checked per country rather than trusted.

Prices are not rounded. 679 × 1.25 = 848.75 and that is what shows. Say the word if you want them rounded to a tidier figure.
The EGP list was checked before anything was built on it: 18,009 prices, zero stray overrides. It stays the reference — the uplift reads from it rather than replacing it, so a base price change still flows through to every market.

Where the difference actually comes from

The +25% adds 399.75 EGP in both foreign markets. Shipping adds 1,020 to Saudi Arabia and 2,320 to Germany — roughly 14× and 30× the Cairo rate. So the courier, not the uplift, is what makes an international order expensive: the uplift is about 15% of the gap to Saudi Arabia and 10% of the gap to Germany.
